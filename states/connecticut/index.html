Recently enacted

Convertible pistols ban (June 2026)

In June 2026, Governor Lamont signed a new law (originating as HB 5043, "An Act Concerning Convertible Pistols") banning the future sale and importation of semiautomatic pistols with a cruciform trigger bar that can be readily converted into a machine gun using a common household tool and an attached "switch." Violating the law is a class D felony.

Source: Governor's press release, June 2026

Permits and purchasing

  • A pistol permit is required to purchase, possess, or carry a pistol or revolver in Connecticut. The permit costs $70 and is valid for five years. Applicants must complete a handgun safety course that includes live fire — the NRA's "Basic Pistol Course" is the minimum standard; dry-fire-only or simulator courses don't qualify.

    Source: CT DESPP — State Pistol Permit

  • A Long Gun Eligibility Certificate is required to purchase a rifle or shotgun, and a separate Handgun Eligibility Certificate exists for those who want to purchase a pistol without going through the full carry-permit process. Both simply authorize purchase and possession, not carrying.

    Source: CT DESPP — Pistol Permit FAQ

  • Connecticut uses a two-step process for a first-time pistol permit: a 60-day temporary permit issued by your local police chief or first selectman, followed by the 5-year state permit issued by DESPP's Special Licensing and Firearms Unit. Renewals go directly to DESPP.

    Source: CT DESPP — Firearms and Permit Forms

  • Permit holders must notify DESPP of an address change within two business days.

    Source: CT DESPP — Pistol Permit FAQ

Safe storage law

Conn. Gen. Stat. § 29-37i

  • State law requires anyone who stores or keeps a firearm to either keep it in a securely locked container/location, or keep it on their person (or close enough to immediately retrieve and use), if they know or reasonably should know that: a minor could access it without a parent/guardian's permission, a resident of the home is legally ineligible to possess a firearm, or a resident poses a risk of imminent injury to themselves or others.

    Source: Conn. Gen. Stat. § 29-37i, current text — statute index also listed at CT Judicial Branch Law Library

  • "Minor" is currently defined as anyone under 18 (this was raised from under-16 by a 2019 amendment).

    Source: Conn. Gen. Stat. § 29-37i, current text

Magazine capacity

  • A "large capacity magazine" is defined as any magazine, belt, drum, feed strip, or similar device that holds — or can be readily restored/converted to hold — more than 10 rounds.

    Source: CT General Assembly, Office of Legislative Research — 2024-R-0163

  • The sale and purchase ban took effect April 5, 2013. Magazines lawfully owned before that date had to be declared to DESPP by January 1, 2014 to remain legally possessed.

    Source: CT General Assembly, Office of Legislative Research — 2024-R-0163

Assault weapons law

Conn. Gen. Stat. § 53-202a et seq.

  • State law generally prohibits possessing, selling, or transferring an "assault weapon" as defined by statute, with an exception for owners who lawfully possessed the firearm before the April 4, 2013 ban and obtained a certificate of possession from DESPP.

    Source: CT General Assembly, Office of Legislative Research — 2013-R-0241, corroborated by DESPP's FAQ on P.A. 13-3

  • The statutory definition covers: any selective-fire firearm capable of automatic, semi-automatic, or burst fire; more than 150 specifically named semiautomatic rifles and pistols; and semiautomatic firearms meeting certain feature-based tests (e.g., a detachable magazine that attaches outside the pistol grip, combined with other listed features).

    Source: CT General Assembly, Office of Legislative Research — 2013-R-0241
